Working with projects

← Back to Projects

Every job lives on its own project. Start one from the Projects page, and everything else (files, bids, the timeline, invoices) hangs off it.

1Find a project

Open Project Overview in the sidebar to see every project as a card. Each card shows the progress, the contract amount, how much has been spent, the finish month and the project manager.

  • Use the tabs (Active, Onboarding, On Hold, Completed, Archived) to narrow the list.
  • Type in Search projects to find one by name, code or address.
  • The tag in the top right of a card tells you if the job is on plan, behind, or how many items are overdue.
  • Click a card to open the project.

2Start a new project

Click + New project. The setup takes four short steps, and you can skip any of them and come back later.

  • Job Name: the project name and a short project code, like HV.
  • Job Site Address: street, city, state and zip.
  • Owner & Lender: the legal owner entity, owner contact, county and lender.
  • Contract & Start: a cover photo, the contract value, the start date and the permit number.
Set the project code early

Incident reports, back charges and VPOs are numbered from the project code (like BC-HV-001). They can't be created until the project has one.

To change any of this later, click the ⋮ button on the project's card, or Edit on its Overview.

3Change a project's status

Click the status tag on a card (or next to the name on the Overview), pick the new status and save. Moving a job to Archived takes it out of the normal list; you'll still find it under the Archived tab.

4Read the Overview

Opening a project lands you on its Overview. Nothing on it is typed in by hand. The numbers come from the timeline, invoices, bids and vendor paperwork, so they stay up to date on their own.

  • Schedule: how much of the work is done compared to the plan, and the projected finish.
  • Spent: invoices so far against the contract (plus any VPOs).
  • Open tasks: tasks still open on the timeline, and how many are overdue.
  • Draw: the latest draw and its status.
  • Timeline: each phase on a month-by-month strip, with a red line for today.

Further down, Needs attention lists what someone should deal with (overdue tasks, expired COIs, bids that are due, invoices without a budget line). Coming up shows the next two weeks. On the right you'll find the money, the latest photos and recent activity.

Moving around a project

The sidebar on a project has a tab for each part of the job: Overview, Project Files, Form Logs, Bids, Project Timeline, To-do, Accounts / Contacts and Accounting. The magnifying glass at the top searches everything on the project at once.

5On your phone

On a phone, the project tabs move to a bar at the bottom of the screen. The round camera button lets you take site photos or pick them from your library. They're saved to the project's 09 Marketing › Photography folder.

6Archive and offload old projects

When a job is done, set it to Completed, and later to Archived. A year after it's archived, IT and Super Admins see a banner on its Overview with a Download project archive button. That builds one zip with every file and record, ready to save on a hard drive.

Nothing gets deleted

After the download you click I saved it to mark it offloaded. The project stays in the portal either way.

?Questions

Why does a card say “On plan” or “Behind”?That compares the timeline to the plan someone locked on the Project Timeline. If no plan is locked yet, you'll see how many items are overdue instead.
How do I delete a project?Open it with Edit. Delete is at the bottom of the setup, and you have to type the project's name to confirm. It can't be undone, so archive it instead unless it was made by mistake.
